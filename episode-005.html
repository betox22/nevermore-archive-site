Sources & further reading

The day after 2 September 1752 was labelled 14 September: eleven nominal dates were omitted once. Existing obligations covered by section 6 were protected by natural elapsed days. The modern rent, office, payroll, contract and school scenes are fictional counterfactual comedy, not testimony about people in 1752 or a universal modern wages rule.

Five-act narration

Act 1 · 0.00–1.29 min

What if payday lost eleven dates?

Imagine our office importing Britain's calendar reset.

Pip opens his planner. Brace for small disappointments.

Yesterday: September second. Today: the fourteenth.

Eleven labels gone. Just one normal night's sleep.

Rex arrives. He smells a chance for profit.

If those dates disappeared, surely Pip's wages disappeared too?

He hands over the check, all bedside charm.

The paycheck is now fun-sized.

Dates vanish only when Rex has to pay.

When he's collecting rent, the very same calendar accelerates.

The fourteenth has arrived. Rex would like cash now.

Pip's pockets: three coins and some old lint.

Rex takes the coins. Despair needs a deposit.

Mia watches. Her face starts to look dangerous.

Britain, seventeen fifty-two: eleven date labels omitted.

Rex counters that company policy outranks reading the statute.

He attaches another late fee to Pip's delivery crate.

The crate had time left. Rex deletes that.

Timmy spots the flaw every respectable apprentice would exploit.

Dates gone? Timmy thinks school should vanish too.

Mia opens the law. It seems Rex-proof.

Act 2 · 1.29–3.88 min

Timmy approaches the exit with the confidence of graduation.

Rex catches his collar before the ceremony can begin.

Time travel: yet one more perk for management.

Timmy gets a broom and eleven fake shifts.

Forced back to work, he improves the boss's system.

If date labels are optional, why preserve next week?

His sponge sends the calendar straight toward Halloween.

Rex likes this. Pip gets an October bill.

The stapler drops. His brain still files this.

Rex also announces that lunch occurred on deleted time.

Pip has apparently eaten retrospectively.

He rushes the freight cart, desperate to avoid another fee.

Timmy's sponge trail introduces the cart to sideways movement.

The boxes discover gravity directly above Pip's left boot.

Pip hops. History hurts right here and now.

Rex steps over him to record the resulting delay.

Rex saves cash by making other people panic.

Timmy does a dead-on copy of the boss.

Rex turns. The copy learns to stand straight.

Another fee. Rex thinks flattery ought to pay.

Timmy objects: how can anyone owe a nonexistent week?

Rex taps his watch, which provides no useful testimony.

Mia reads the law behind this whole mess.

She finds a clause that looks suspiciously Rex-shaped.

Rex sees her grin and claims the book.

Mia keeps her place. The book is not surrendering.

First, she asks Rex to explain his logic.

He claims Parliament erased eleven actual days.

So, he thinks, the firm can erase debts.

Pip looks up, hoping Mia has brought better mathematics.

Timmy leans on his broom. What about him?

Rex stamps the new policy with entirely unearned authority.

The ink hits his cuff. Even stationery has objections.

He points Pip toward the remaining mountain of boxes.

Pip tries lifting one. The deadline grows heavier somehow.

Mia steps between the worker and the boss's revelation.

Did the sun, she asks, skip eleven sunrises overnight?

Rex consults the uncooperative window.

Moving paper cannot fast-forward the planet or this afternoon.

Rex thinks the stars should serve his pay clerk.

Mia opens section one of the actual calendar statute.

Parliament changed date labels. It did not remove lived time.

September second. Next day, the fourteenth.

She turns to section six. Here comes the expensive part.

Act 3 · 3.88–6.41 min

The year's new beginning? January first.

Eleven dates omitted. Once, that September.

Time went on. The hourglass got no promotion.

Rex insists the numbers still justify his early demands.

That is precisely the sort of problem section six addressed.

Those existing rent and payment terms kept natural time.

Their deadlines ran as if nothing changed.

Existing lease terms did not shrink with the printed calendar.

The red pen stops negotiating.

Pip's wallet finally receives some encouraging news from history.

Listed ages and service terms kept natural time.

Rex can't make extra work from erased labels.

Timmy asks whether this rescues his attempted school holiday.

In our fake office, Mia uses common sense.

School still exists. A tragedy.

Rex claims offices like his get special treatment.

Mia asks whose rulebook started this mess.

You can't take skipped dates and ditch safeguards.

His forehead begins calculating the cost of that observation.

Mia collects the fees Rex dreamed up from dates.

The clock ticks on, without asking the board.

Pip approaches with the reduced paycheck and the injured boot.

If no working time vanished, where did his money go?

Rex discovers the floor's fascinating grain.

Mia points back to the protection of existing obligations.

The agreed delivery date did not jump forward.

Down goes the crate. Pip's boot welcomes this.

Next: Rex's October bill. Timmy's sponge aged badly.

Protected rent doesn't speed up with new labels.

Mia tears the fake bill. Proof turns festive.

Rex tries saving the scraps before they reach his inkwell.

His elbow starts an audit of liquid assets.

The ink audits every column of his calendar deductions.

Timmy likes books that look like real life.

Rex reaches for the blotter and meets the forgotten sponge.

He lands in the chair. The boss goes mobile.

The chair spins past Pip. A boss on wheels.

Pip takes his coins back from Rex's tour.

Rex hits the calendar. It declines to meet.

Halloween becomes a management hat.

Pip grabs the lunch Rex stole through dates.

The recovered sandwich tastes strongly of surviving one's supervisor.

Mia brings a check. Rex supplies his name.

The hat lends the scene one last scrap of grace.

Act 4 · 6.41–7.71 min

His disappearing-payday scheme ends with a very real check.

Off comes the hat. The crowd stays. Bad luck.

He flees to save what pride he has left.

The door refuses a dramatic exit.

Rex sets the clock. Who says he lacks power?

The spring answers by resigning from its position immediately.

Mia puts the real dates back in place.

The labels skip; the people never skipped living between sunsets.

Pip mends his boot. Jargon won't help here.

Back to sums, Timmy has cause for doubt.

He wonders whether skipped labels could at least accelerate adulthood.

Those listed legal ages still used natural days.

He celebrates another doomed shortcut by balancing the broom.

Mia catches it before Pip needs a second protective boot.

The delivery cart is loaded at an ordinary human pace.

No fake due date should launch those crates.

Rex peeks through blinds. Has sane work failed?

His broken clock remains impressively consistent.

Time goes on. The clock has quit its job.

Rex realizes he cannot fire the rotation of Earth.

He opens a clean ledger and restores the actual deadlines.

The books track time. They don't try to fake it.

Act 5 · 7.71–9.00 min

Next day, Pip owes no extra month's rent.

Rex brings a new clock, not a new scheme.

He hangs it beside the calendar, allowing both to coexist.

Pip pays the agreed rent when it is actually due.

Rex signs the receipt. The stamp remains under supervision.

Timmy gives Mia his sums. No dates erased.

She adds a star. Apparently actual work still counts.

Pip signs for the goods on the due date.

No lived days vanished, in law or our farce.

Listed terms kept natural time; only labels changed.

Wages, deadlines, sanity. The office gets them back.

Of course, Rex still searches for discounts inside the calendar.

He notices the historical change to New Year's beginning.

Two New Years. Surely that means two fees?

Mia clears her throat. History has retained legal counsel.

Rex closes the planner. Innocently.

Pip locks up the check before Rex thinks again.

Timmy sweeps up the dust from the calendar revolution.

The new clock ticks through an entirely ordinary afternoon.

Labels change. Your life is not a spare page.

Try erasing payday again, Rex. We kept the paperwork.

Labels jump. Greed stays punctual.

Original approved narration

00–05s From The Raven Archive...

05–15s One of the biggest entertainment franchises on Earth only exists because a programmer accidentally turned the police into homicidal maniacs.

15–30s In 1995, a small Scottish studio called DMA Design was building a top-down driving game called Race'n'Chase. You could play as a cop or a criminal, but the rules were painfully strict—if you ran a red light or scratched a bumper, you lost points. It was slow, predictable, and frankly, boring.

30–46s Then, a glitch broke the police pathfinding code. Instead of pulling players over, cop cars locked onto the player's exact coordinates and rammed them at full speed until their car exploded. The programmers were about to fix the bug—until they noticed the playtesters couldn't stop laughing.

46–62s So they scrapped the good-cop concept, made you the criminal, and hired Britain's most notorious publicist, Max Clifford, to leak outrage to the tabloids. Politicians condemned the game in Parliament, newspapers called it a menace to society, and millions of gamers rushed out to buy it.

62–75s That single coding mistake evolved into the 3D revolution of GTA III and a multi-billion-dollar empire. Proof that in game design, sometimes the best feature is a massive mistake.
